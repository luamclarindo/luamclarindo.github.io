Hypercuíca is an augmented cuíca, an Afro-Brazilian friction drum, developed through a series of experimental systems that explore the relationships between bodily gesture, acoustic sound, digital processing and machine co-improvisation.

A clip-on microphone captures the cuíca's acoustic sound. The signal is processed in Ableton Live and returned through loudspeakers, while the instrument's acoustic voice remains audible throughout. From system 2 onwards, a smartphone attached to the drum provides motion data: tilt and left–right rotation are sent to the computer over OSC and mapped to sound-processing parameters.

This page follows the development of the instrument through six systems, from early MIDI control to motion-based interaction and a machine co-improviser. The systems are presented chronologically; they are not a sequence of replacements, but different configurations that test distinct relationships between gesture and sound.

Gesture vocabulary

From system 2 onwards, two axes of the phone's gravity signal are used: up/down tilt and left/right rotation. Continuous mappings respond across a range of movement; toggles are triggered when a gesture reaches an extreme. The exact thresholds and mappings vary between systems.

↑ Tilt upMove the top of the instrument upward. Used for continuous timbral control and, in some systems, mode switching.
↓ Tilt downMove the top of the instrument downward. Used for minimum values, delay activation or stochastic reconfiguration.
↺ Rotate leftRotate the instrument to the left. Depending on the system, controls reverb, the Shifter or other parameters.
↻ Rotate rightRotate the instrument to the right. Depending on the system, controls delay, resonators or spatial processing.

Six systems

System 0 · Before the PhD

Controller, footswitch and Ableton Live

MIDI controller + footswitch → Ableton Live

The earliest configuration amplified the cuíca into Ableton Live, where sound was shaped by hand and foot. Knobs on a MIDI controller provided continuous control, while a footswitch triggered a toggle. One press froze the delay and handed control of its left and right delay times to an LFO. The two times moved in opposite directions, so the frozen echo continued to drift rather than remaining static.

Cuíca + microphone→Ableton Live
Delay · LFO · Auto Pan→Loudspeakers
FootswitchToggle: freeze the delay and activate the LFO behaviour.
MIDI controllerContinuous knobs and MIDI notes for effect parameters.

In the recorded mapping, the LFO moves the left delay time from 88% to 22% and the right delay time from 19% to 81%.

System 1 · With Test Death, Bragança

Novation controller, footswitch and phone light

Novation + footswitch + phone light → plugdata inside Live

Played with the band Test Death in Bragança, this configuration combined two parallel control layers. A Novation controller and footswitch acted directly on Ableton Live's MIDI mappings. A phone's light sensor, read through ZIG SIM and OSC, controlled the frequency of an oscillator inside a plugdata patch. The oscillator was multiplied with the cuíca input, producing amplitude/ring modulation and a metallic, sideband-rich timbre. The patch also included routing for gravity data to a drive stage and a threshold-triggered table looper.

Novation + footswitch→Live MIDI mappings
Phone light + motion→ZIG SIM / OSC→plugdata inside Live→Sound processing
Control sourceFunction
Novation / footswitchDelay wet and feedback, freeze, LFO, Shifter, ring modulation, looper repeat and return sends.
Phone lightScaled to oscillator frequency (approximately 100–500 Hz), modulating the cuíca input.
Phone gravityRouted in plugdata to a tanh drive stage and threshold-triggered table recording/playback.

System 2 · PhD project defence, University of Aveiro, 2025

The defence patch

ZIG SIM gravity → Max for Live

This was the first configuration to bring the phone's gravity sensor and the main control logic into a single Max for Live patch. The Shifter remained active, while gesture continuously shaped drive, envelope following, reverb and delay. Upward tilt increased a drive/reduction macro combining Redux, Drum Buss, saturation, compression and fine pitch detuning. Rotation controlled the Shifter's envelope-follow amount across the sweep, with a reverb wash growing to the left and delay/feedback growing to the right. The only toggle was the Hybrid Reverb freeze.

↑ Tilt upContinuous drive and reduction: Redux, Drum Buss, saturation, glue makeup and fine pitch.
↺ Rotate leftIncreases the Hybrid Reverb wash.
↻ Rotate rightIncreases delay and feedback.
Whole rotation sweepControls the Shifter envelope follower (−24 to +24 semitones); Shifter stays on.
FreezeToggle for Hybrid Reverb freeze.
Phone gravity→ZIG SIM · OSC→Max for Live→Live macros

System 3 · NIME 2026

The NIME mapping

Gravity → Max for Live · three module toggles + two continuous macros

Presented in the paper “Hypercuíca: Augmenting and Performing with an Afro-Brazilian Friction Drum” (NIME 2026, London), this mapping combines structural switches with continuous control. A full left rotation toggles the Shifter, a full right rotation toggles the Resonators, and a full downward tilt toggles the Delay. Between these extremes, the gestures continuously shape the active transformations.

↺ Full leftToggle: Shifter on/off.
↻ Full rightToggle: Resonators on/off.
↓ Full tilt downToggle: Delay on/off.
↑ Tilt upContinuous macro across delay, feedback, reverb, resonator decay, Shifter, EQ, saturation and gain.
Rotation sweepContinuous macro across panning, Shifter pitch, delay time and LFO amount.

The mappings were refined iteratively for ergonomic use, gestural clarity and expressive continuity. They describe a functional configuration rather than a prescriptive or definitive mapping.

System 4 · Frictionary · Intermediartes 2026

A vocabulary of gesture-based sonic modulations

Natural ↔ Radical · continuous gestures + stochastic reconfiguration

Frictionary joins “friction” (how the cuíca makes sound) and “dictionary” (a named vocabulary of gestures). It was used to record an album and was not performed publicly. The system introduces two processing modes and two interaction types.

GestureActionModeResult
Tilt downDrive itNatural · continuousIncreases saturation.
Rotate leftCave itNatural · continuousIncreases reverb.
Rotate rightEcho itNatural · continuousControls delay.
Tilt up (~75°)Flip itGlobal · toggleSwitches between Natural and Radical.
Tilt down (~75°)Topsy-turvyGlobal · toggleRe-rolls a random effect chain, parameter start values and gesture assignments.
Tilt or rotateGhost itRadical · continuousModulates randomly assigned parameters.

Natural mode extends the instrument's qualities through continuous saturation, reverb and delay. Radical mode introduces stochastic processes. Each downward Topsy-turvy gesture re-rolls three layers: which effects are active (up to four of Grain Delay, Shifter, Resonator and Pitch), their initial parameter values, and which parameters are assigned to tilt or rotation. The gesture therefore does not only control the sound; it renegotiates its own control structure. Tilting up returns to Natural mode.

System 5 · Flowfal + Somax2

Motion control and a machine co-improviser

Ready-made plugins · no custom Max for Live programming

This configuration replaces ZIG SIM with FlowfalMap devices and introduces Somax2 as a machine co-improviser. Two FlowfalMap instances read the phone's Roll (rotation) and Pitch (tilt). The cuíca feeds two Ableton Live tracks, an Influencer and a Player, which drive Somax2. Each track has its own convolution reverb: an Arena hall of about 20 seconds on the Influencer and larger spaces on the Player.

↻ Rotate rightRaises reverb decay and wet level on both tracks, while moving their panning controls in opposite directions.
↑ Tilt upRaises Somax2's probability parameter, so the machine responds more often.

The corpus contains 20 minutes of cuíca improvisation recorded with the same microphone, favouring slow, whale-like sonorities over repetitive material. The spacious source material shapes the character of the machine's responses. A current limitation is that Somax2 for Live builds its corpus offline; using Somax2 for Max to build the corpus while playing is a next step to explore.

Cuíca microphone→Influencer + Player→Somax2→Output

Across the systems

Systems 2 to 5 share the same two gesture axes, but the role of gesture changes. In system 2 it is a continuous controller of timbre. In system 3, the extremes become structural switches while the middle range remains continuous. In system 4, gesture also reassigns its own control structure. In system 5, tilt influences how often a machine improviser responds, while rotation shapes the acoustic space.

Current performance configuration

In the most recent performance configuration documented here, two Ableton Live tracks run in parallel: one uses the NIME mapping (system 3), and the other uses the earlier defence mapping (system 2). The module toggles of system 3 and the always-on Shifter, grit, envelope, reverb and delay behaviour of system 2 act on the cuíca together.
